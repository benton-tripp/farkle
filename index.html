Scoring Rules

Change scoring and turn rules

Assumptions and limits

The dice are fair and independent. Each combination must come from one roll. The model evaluates one player's current turn, without opponents, final-round rules, exact-score finishes, or piggybacking.

Roll probabilities come from exact outcome counts. Expected values and turn-level probabilities use dynamic programming with floating-point arithmetic. High-score states use a derived boundary that respects the selected rules, including forced hot-dice rerolls. There is no arbitrary instruction to bank at a score cap.

Moves are ranked by the selected objective. Near-equal floating-point results are treated as ties. For target-probability ties, expected points come next; remaining ties favor lower Farkle risk, then banking.

Three pairs require three different faces. A six-of-a-kind roll doesn't receive the two-distinct-triplets award, but it can be scored as two ordinary triplets. Custom rules that require a named combination to override a higher partition score aren't modeled.

Input scores must be multiples of 50, up to 100,000. The calculator doesn't track turns or update your score automatically. Read the derivation and boundary proof.

Scores and settings are saved in this browser. Calculations stay on this device. MathJax loads from jsDelivr.
